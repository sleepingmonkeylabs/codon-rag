# Vimalakīrti — Chapter 1: Purification of the Buddhafield
*Paraphrase of Toh 176 (Thurman/84000)*

---

**The scene.** Buddha is teaching in a grove outside Vaiśālī. A large gathering — monks, bodhisattvas, gods, the usual crowd — all seasoned practitioners, all in good shape.

Five hundred young men from the city arrive with jewelled parasols as offerings, each laying one down before the Buddha. Immediately — through Buddha's power — all the parasols merge into one vast canopy stretching across the entire universe. Inside it the whole of existence is reflected: suns, moons, heavens, oceans, cities, wilderness. All the Buddhas in every direction can be heard teaching. Everyone goes quiet. It's remarkable.

---

**The question.** One of the young men, Ratnakara, sings the Buddha's praises and then asks the question that drives the entire chapter:

> *"What do you actually mean when you say a bodhisattva purifies his buddhafield?"*

---

**The answer.** Buddha explains that a buddhafield is a mindscape — it arises in proportion to how deeply a bodhisattva works with living beings. Every inner quality corresponds to the kind of beings born in that field. The key line:

> *"The purity of his buddhafield reflects the purity of living beings. The purity of living beings reflects the purity of his gnosis. The purity of his gnosis reflects the purity of his mind."*

The chain runs: **mind → gnosis → beings → field.** Not separate things.

---

**Śāriputra's silent objection.** He thinks to himself — nothing said out loud:

> *"If the field's purity reflects the bodhisattva's mind, then Śākyamuni's mind must have been pretty rough when he was practicing. Because this field doesn't look particularly pure to me."*

Buddha reads the thought.

---

**The exchange.**

**Buddha:** If a person blind from birth can't see the sun — is the sun impure?

**Śāriputra:** No. The fault is in the blind person, not the sun.

**Buddha:** Exactly. The fact that you see what you see is not the field's problem.

*Brahma Sikhin cuts in:*

**Brahma:** I see this field as splendid as the finest divine realms.

**Śāriputra:** I see thorns. Uneven ground. Filth.

**Brahma:** That's because there are highs and lows in your mind. Those who meet all beings with impartiality see this field as perfectly pure.

---

**The demonstration.** Buddha presses his big toe into the ground.

The entire universe instantly transforms into a mass of jewels and lotus thrones. Everyone in the assembly finds themselves seated on gem-encrusted seats.

**Śāriputra:** I have never seen anything like this.

**Buddha:** This is how it always is. I let it appear rough to suit the beings who need that. Like gods eating from the same vessel but each tasting something different according to their merits — beings see the field's beauty in proportion to their own degree of purity.

He lifts his toe. Everything returns to how it looked before.

Thirty-two thousand have their Dharma-eye opened. Eight thousand monks are freed.

---

*Same world, two modes of seeing. The field doesn't change. The mind projecting the impurity is both the problem and the solution.*
