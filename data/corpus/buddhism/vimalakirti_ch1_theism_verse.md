## THE THEISM PROBLEM IN CHAPTER 1

**Your observation:** The line "I let it appear rough to suit the beings who need that" installs Buddha as cosmic stage manager — a divine agent deciding what the set looks like. Doctrinally incoherent given the teaching being delivered.

**The contradiction:** The lesson is "impurity is in your mind, not the world" — delivered via a miracle performed by an omnipotent external agent. The demonstration undermines the teaching it illustrates.

**Nyingma reframe:** Remove the agent entirely. Appearances self-arise in accordance with the perceiver's condition. Nobody authors it.

> "Appearances arise in accordance with the minds present. Rough minds, rough field. The same place — no one arranging it."

**The toe-press reread:** Not Buddha doing something to the world — Buddha suspending the assembly's perceptual filter momentarily. The jewel-field doesn't appear *instead of* the rough field. It appears *as* the rough field, filter lifted. When he lifts the toe, rough field returns — because Śāriputra's karmic vision reasserts. Nothing changed in the world. Same structure as the pointing-out instruction: the teacher presses your toe. You see it for a moment. Ordinary mind reasserts.

**Brahma Sikhin's role:** He sees the jewel-field before the demonstration — evidence it is already present for those whose filter is thinner. The toe-press temporarily democratises what Brahma has continuously. Two resolutions of the same signal, co-present for everyone in the room.

## THE EYE-PRESS THAT WASN'T

**Your suggestion:** Buddha could have pressed Śāriputra's eye instead — "You make this appear filthy."

**The distinction:** Toe-press addresses the assembly. Eye-press addresses the mechanism. One is demonstration, the other is diagnosis.

**Why Vimalakīrti never uses the toe-press:** He goes straight for the load-bearing assumption in the specific person in front of him:
- Śāriputra sitting in meditation → *that's* not meditation
- Flowers sticking to Śāriputra → *that's* your discriminating mind
- Śāriputra wondering about seating → *you came for the Dharma or for a chair?*

Every intervention: locate the specific reification in this specific person, press *that*.

Chapter 1 is Buddha still working in the grand register — cosmic canopies, jewel-fields. By the time Vimalakīrti takes over, the method tightens completely. No more spectacle. Just: *what exactly are you holding onto right now?*

**Structural corollary:** Vimalakīrti can press the eye precisely because he operates outside the institutional register that occasionally requires jewel-fields. A monk-teacher has obligations to the genre. Vimalakīrti has none.

**On the toe-press as upāya:** Mipham's escape hatch — conventionally valid for beings who need the dramatic version — is polite about the fact that the conventional version is doing heavy theological lifting in a text that purports to be about non-conceptual seeing.

**Summary verdict:** "It is doctrinally awful." Confirmed.

## TIBETAN VERSE — CHAPTER 1 ENCODED

Composed as four-line Tibetan verse encoding the doctrinal content of Chapter 1 through a Dzogchen lens.

### U-chen

སེམས་ཉིད་ཀ་དག་སྤྲོས་བྲལ་ཀློང་།
བླ་མའི་རྐྱེན་གྱིས་རྣམ་རྟོག་རོལ།
ཉོན་མོངས་རང་བཞིན་ཐུགས་རྗེའི་རྩལ།
བྱང་སེམས་འགྲོ་བའི་འདམ་དུ་འཇུག།

### Wylie

*sems nyid ka dag spros bral klong /*
*bla ma'i rkyen gyis rnam rtog rol /*
*nyon mongs rang bzhin thugs rje'i rtsal /*
*byang sems 'gro ba'i 'dam du 'jug /*

### English mapping

1. The nature of mind is the primordially pure, unelaborated expanse.
2. Triggered by the lama's presence, discursive thought manifests as play.
3. The nature of defilements is the energy of responsiveness.
4. Bodhicitta enters the mud of transmigrating beings.

---

## NOTES ON THE VERSE

**Lines 1 & 3** — doctrinally clean Dzogchen. Ka dag and spros bral slightly redundant but work as verse intensification. Rang bzhin in line 3 does real work: not "afflictions transformed into compassion" but "afflictions whose nature *is* compassion-energy." No alchemy required.

**Lines 2 & 4** — encode the sūtra's own unresolved residue. Line 2: *rkyen* (condition/catalyst) rather than agent — the lama's presence is the condition under which rnam rtog reveals itself as rol-pa. Defensible, though rnam rtog rol risks reading as discursive thought playing around rather than discursive thought recognised as play. Line 4: *'jug* (enters) retains externalist residue — bodhicitta arriving into mud from outside. The mud and bodhicitta remain two things.

**Pedagogical use:** The verse maps Chapter 1's tension exactly. Lines 1 and 3 are the Dzogchen resolution. Lines 2 and 4 encode the unresolved theistic residue. Students can be asked: *where does the agent still hide?*
